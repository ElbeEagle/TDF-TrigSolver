\title{A Meta-Model-Guided Symbolic Reasoning Method for Solving Trigonometric Function Problems}

\begin{abstract}
Automated trigonometric function problem solving is a pivotal yet demanding task in intelligent education systems, requiring strict adherence to angle conventions, domain-conditioned equivalence, multi-branch coverage, and infinite periodic completeness. While existing formal systems, large language models, and computer algebra systems can execute individual symbolic operations, they lack a domain-specific control mechanism to determine operation applicability, track deductive state evolution, or ensure that local derivations satisfy global answer contracts. In this paper, we propose a Meta-Model-Guided Symbolic Reasoning Framework. We first formalize domain-specific operations into structured, verifiable units termed \textit{Trigonometric Meta-Models} (TMMs), which encapsulate algebraic prerequisites, bounded operations, state update rules, and local validation contracts. We then develop a dynamic \textit{Decoupled Inference Strategy} (DIS) that explores validated state transitions over a candidate priority queue, coordinating four cross-task operations: normalization, equivalence transformation, branch reasoning, and periodic completion. Evaluated on a frozen 50-problem benchmark against Direct LLM and LLM + generic CAS baselines, our method achieves \textit{[EVIDENCE NEEDED: correct/50 and percentage]} accuracy, with targeted ablations demonstrating the indispensable role of periodic lifting and exact validation. The results substantiate the efficacy of a bounded, inspectable symbolic reasoning process for educational mathematics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Automatic mathematical problem solving serves as a foundational technology for intelligent tutoring and automated assessment systems, where a solver must deliver not only exact final answers but also inspectable, mathematically sound derivation steps \cite{yu2022func-relation, lu_radarmath_2021}. Direct trigonometric function problems represent a particularly rigorous challenge in this domain. A single problem may query exact values, algebraic simplifications, sinusoidal properties, or periodic solution sets. In each scenario, intermediate transformations remain valid only under strict mathematical conditions, such as non-zero denominators in tangent expansions or designated interval boundaries. Consequently, solving procedures easily fall into a ``locally plausible yet globally fallacious'' trap: a derivation may inadvertently shift angle units, drop domain restrictions, omit valid equation branches, or truncate infinite periodic solutions to a single base cycle. Achieving reliable problem solving therefore demands an explicitly controlled symbolic process rather than isolated algebraic calculations.

A robust trigonometric reasoning process must satisfy four mutually coupled requirements. First, \textit{angle and expression normalization} must standardize diverse notations into canonical forms without erasing stated domain constraints or variable bindings. Second, \textit{equivalence-preserving transformation} must guarantee algebraic equivalence strictly over the active domain rather than pursuing superficial visual simplification. Third, \textit{constraint and branch reasoning} must retain all valid solution branches, boundary endpoints, and singularity exclusions. Fourth, \textit{periodic completion and validation} must lift base-period results to the requested global domain and verify structural answer contracts. Crucially, these requirements form an interconnected chain: an algebraic identity may expose the precise branches required for interval reasoning, whereas losing a single branch in the base period irreversibly invalidates the subsequent periodic lifting. Simply appending periodic suffixes (e.g., $+2k\pi$) as post-hoc textual formatting cannot rectify semantic omissions incurred during earlier steps.

Existing methodologies only address isolated segments of this reasoning chain. Formal trigonometric systems offer rigorous proof steps within restricted formal languages \cite{liu2022autotrig, xiong2023trigo}, yet their rigid rule sets struggle to generalize across diverse problem types involving equations, properties, and inequalities. Large language models (LLMs) provide broad natural-language coverage and problem interpretation \cite{hendrycks2021math, zhao2024ssccot}, but their generative nature lacks symbolic soundness, frequently hallucinating non-equivalent transformations or incomplete branch sets. Integrating LLMs with generic Computer Algebra Systems (CAS) improves raw computational power \cite{meurer2017sympy}; however, generic CAS tools act as passive executors. They assume that a well-formed mathematical query is already available and typically return only principal branches or unlifted base solutions. Connecting an LLM to a CAS does not inherently specify when an operator is applicable, how intermediate states should evolve, or how to validate global completeness. The fundamental gap is therefore not a shortage of individual mathematical operators, but the absence of a domain-specific control strategy that dynamically composes bounded, verifiable actions based on the evolving reasoning state.

To address this challenge, this paper presents a meta-model-guided symbolic reasoning framework for direct trigonometric function problems, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:reasoning_overview}. Following the established two-phase solving paradigm \cite{yu2022func-relation, yu2026udfsolver}, the framework first employs a lightweight problem-understanding front end to map raw text into a mathematically grounded \textit{understood state} ($U^{(0)}$), isolating linguistic parsing from symbolic deduction. From this understood state, our reasoning engine operates through two core mechanisms. First, we formulate \textit{Trigonometric Meta-Models} (TMMs) as reusable, guarded symbolic actions, where each model couples algebraic preconditions, an executable CAS operation, state transition rules, and a local validation contract. Second, we design a dynamic \textit{Decoupled Inference Strategy} (DIS) that navigates the state transition graph via a priority-guided search policy, validating each step and backtracking upon failure until a verified global answer is achieved or an explicit abstention is returned.

The primary contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Formalization of Trigonometric Meta-Models (TMMs):} We establish a structured 5-tuple formulation for trigonometric operations that explicitly pairs operational prerequisites with local verification contracts, preventing invalid transformations and branch omissions at the step level.
    \item \textbf{A Dynamic Decoupled Inference Strategy (DIS):} We develop a state-conditioned inference strategy that decouples global search control from domain-specific execution, dynamically coordinating normalization, identity transformation, branch reasoning, and periodic completion.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Benchmark and Targeted Empirical Analysis:} We conduct an end-to-end evaluation on a frozen 50-problem benchmark against Direct LLM and LLM + generic CAS baselines. Targeted ablation studies quantify the critical necessity of periodic completion and exact symbolic validation in eliminating false acceptances.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section~\ref{sec:related_work} reviews related work in formal math reasoning and tool-augmented LLMs; Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} formalizes the problem definition and understood state; Section~\ref{sec:method} details the TMM library and dynamic DIS algorithm; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} presents the experimental setup, baseline comparisons, and ablation analyses; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses theoretical insights and scope boundaries; and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Formal Trigonometric Reasoning}

Research dedicated to trigonometric reasoning has largely examined restricted formal tasks. AutoTrig searches over normalized expressions and transformation rules for identity proving \cite{liu2022autotrig}, while TRIGO studies generative proof reduction with step-level verification in Lean \cite{xiong2023trigo}. Process-oriented benchmarks such as SSC-CoT and TriMaster100 further examine the consistency of language-model derivations on complex trigonometric problems \cite{zhao2024ssccot}. These studies establish the value of bounded rules and verifiable steps. Their central tasks, however, do not jointly cover property derivation, interval constraints, multiple equation branches, and complete periodic answer contracts. The present work retains bounded symbolic actions but studies how heterogeneous actions can be composed within one problem-level reasoning process.

\subsection{Language Models and Computer Algebra}

Broad mathematical benchmarks, including MATH, MathVista, MathVerse, CMM-Math, and UGMathBench, show the increasing scope of language and multimodal models \cite{hendrycks2021math,lu2024mathvista,zhang2024mathverse,liu2024cmmmath,xu2025ugmathbench}. Generated reasoning is useful for interpreting heterogeneous statements, but sequence plausibility does not guarantee trigonometric equivalence, branch coverage, or set completeness. Computer algebra systems complement language models with exact simplification, equation solving, domain analysis, and symbolic sets \cite{meurer2017sympy}. Nevertheless, a CAS result is conditioned on its input query and may represent only a principal branch or base-period result. Language-model--CAS pipelines therefore remain incomplete when tool selection, domain preservation, periodic lifting, and final acceptance are left implicit.

\subsection{Structured Function Problem Solving}

Relation-centric algorithms for text-diagram function problems separate problem understanding from symbolic solving by first acquiring an understood state from the input \cite{yu2022func-relation}. Subsequent TDF research formalizes uniform states, function meta-models, and a Decoupled Inference Strategy that separates procedural control from function-specific execution \cite{yu2026udfsolver}. We adopt the established understood-state interface and the general decoupling principle rather than claiming them as new concepts. This paper specializes the reasoning layer for trigonometric functions: its TMMs encode angle, equivalence, branch, and periodic prerequisites, while its DIS dynamically explores validated transitions and checks global answer completeness.


\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\subsection{Problem Statement and Output Contract}
Let a raw text-based trigonometric function problem be represented as
\begin{equation}
    P=(t,\mathcal{O}),
\end{equation}
where $t$ denotes the natural-language problem narrative containing mathematical expressions and domain constraints, and $\mathcal{O}$ is an optional candidate set for multiple-choice questions. 

To support rigorous verification and educational transparency, an automated solver must produce a structured mathematical outcome rather than an opaque label prediction. We define the solver output as a 4-tuple:
\begin{equation}
    Y=(a,\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H},z),
\end{equation}
where $a$ represents an exact scalar, evaluated expression, functional property, or matched choice; $\mathcal{S}$ denotes the exact solution set (expressed as an ordinary subset or an infinite periodic set over the reals); $\mathcal{H} = \langle (U^{(0)}, M_1, U^{(1)}), \dots, (U^{(m-1)}, M_m, U^{(m)}) \rangle$ records the complete sequence of validated symbolic transitions; and $z \in \{\texttt{solved}, \texttt{abstain}\}$ indicates the terminal status accompanied by explicit failure attribution when unresolvable. For multiple-choice problems, the solver strictly adheres to a \textit{math-first principle}: it constructs the definitive mathematical solution independently before identifying the equivalent option in $\mathcal{O}$, thereby preventing shortcut guessing.

\subsection{Decoupled Front-End and Understood State}
Directly executing algebraic reasoning on raw natural-language text introduces severe linguistic ambiguity and risks losing implicit mathematical constraints. Following the classical two-phase solving paradigm \cite{yu2022func-relation}, our framework establishes an explicit structural boundary between natural-language understanding and symbolic reasoning via an intermediate state.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 1 (Understood State).}
An intermediate state $U^{(0)}$ is defined as an \textit{understood state} of problem $P$ if it encapsulates all explicit mathematical entities, angle conditions, domain constraints, variable definitions, and target goals required for the symbolic engine to derive the final answer $Y$ without re-examining the raw text $t$.

To reliably establish $U^{(0)}$, a lightweight problem-understanding front end executes two deterministic stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Formula Normalization and Entity Grounding:} Mathematical expressions are parsed into normalized Abstract Syntax Trees (ASTs) while mapping natural-language variable bindings and angle specifications (e.g., $x \in [0, 2\pi]$ or degree-to-radian relations).
    \item \textit{Constrained Semantic Extraction and Verification:} The front end extracts explicit constraints, relational equations, and the target goal into a candidate state $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$. A structural schema validator subsequently verifies reference completeness and operational sufficiency. If an unsupported operator or ambiguous target is detected, the system issues an early explicit abstention.
\end{enumerate}
This decoupled design shields the downstream symbolic engine from textual variations, ensuring that all subsequent deductive steps operate exclusively on mathematically verified representations.

\subsection{Problem Scope and Evaluation Strata}
This study focuses on \textit{direct trigonometric function problems}, where trigonometric functions, composite identities, and periodic properties constitute the primary objects of mathematical inquiry. We delineate five foundational problem strata based on educational curricula:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exact Expression Evaluation:} Computing exact algebraic values for non-trivial angle combinations and composite expressions.
    \item \textbf{Identity Transformation:} Deriving equivalent canonical expressions across product-to-sum, double-angle, and auxiliary-angle identities.
    \item \textbf{Sinusoidal Function Properties:} Determining fundamental analytical characteristics, including period $T$, amplitude $A$, phase shift $\varphi$, symmetry axes, monotonicity intervals, and extrema.
    \item \textbf{Trigonometric Equations:} Solving equations across bounded intervals or constructing complete periodic solution sets over the real domain $\mathbb{R}$.
    \item \textbf{Domain, Range, and Inequalities:} Deducing valid variable domains, function ranges, and solving trigonometric inequalities with boundary and singularity exclusions.
\end{itemize}
These five strata serve to structure our benchmark and evaluate cross-task generalization; the underlying reasoning engine employs a unified, operator-composition mechanism rather than disjoint, category-specific heuristics.


\section{Meta-Model-Guided Symbolic Reasoning}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture Overview}
Navigating multi-step trigonometric derivations requires resolving the tension between local transformation validity and global goal progression. Monolithic rule engines suffer from combinatorial path explosion, while unconstrained Computer Algebra Systems (CAS) often introduce invalid branch cuts or lose domain constraints. To achieve robust, goal-directed, and inspectable reasoning, we propose a two-layer architecture comprising a structured \textit{Trigonometric Meta-Model (TMM) Library} and a dynamic \textit{Decoupled Inference Strategy (DIS)}, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:reasoning_overview}.

\caption{The proposed Meta-Model-Guided Symbolic Reasoning Architecture. A lightweight front end converts raw input $P$ into an understood state $U^{(0)}$. The reasoning core, governed by DIS, dynamically retrieves applicable TMMs from the library $\mathcal{L}$, executes bounded symbolic operations with controlled CAS assistance, enforces dual-gate validations, and traverses the state space to generate a verified answer $Y$ or an explicit abstention.}
\label{fig:reasoning_overview}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:reasoning_overview}, the reasoning process commences from the understood state $U^{(0)}$. DIS maintains a priority-ranked candidate queue and iteratively evaluates the unsatisfied goal against the active state. For each state, DIS retrieves candidate TMMs whose structural and domain preconditions are satisfied. Each TMM executes a bounded transformation via a controlled symbolic executor and commits the successor state only if it passes a local verification check. The search terminates when all goal requirements are satisfied and verified by a global answer validator. By cleanly separating operator execution (TMM) from trajectory control (DIS), the framework guarantees mathematical soundness at each step while maintaining global search efficiency.

\subsection{Trigonometric Meta-Models (TMMs)}
Standard algebraic rewrite rules typically alter expression syntax without verifying underlying domain preconditions (e.g., non-zero denominators in tangent formulas $\tan \theta = \sin \theta / \cos \theta$). To guarantee that every symbolic step is mathematically sound, guarded, and inspectable, we encapsulate domain operations into structured units termed \textit{Trigonometric Meta-Models}.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 2 (Trigonometric Meta-Model).}
A Trigonometric Meta-Model is formalized as a 5-tuple:
\begin{equation}
    M_i = \langle I_i, P_i, O_i, U_i, V_i \rangle,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $I_i$ is a unique functional identifier;
    \item $P_i(U)$ is an applicability predicate defining structural patterns, angle compatibility, and domain validity required on state $U$;
    \item $O_i$ is a bounded, deterministic symbolic operation executed via the controlled CAS interface;
    \item $U_i(U, \widetilde{e})$ is a state transition function that integrates the operation output $\widetilde{e}$ into the reasoning state;
    \item $V_i(U, \widetilde{U})$ is a local validation contract enforcing algebraic equivalence, branch retention, and constraint preservation.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Dual-Gate Guard Mechanism:} A TMM enforces a strict two-tier safety protocol:
\begin{equation}
    U^{(j+1)} = 
    \begin{cases} 
        U_i(U^{(j)}, O_i(U^{(j)})), & \text{if } P_i(U^{(j)}) \land V_i(U^{(j)}, \widetilde{U}) = \text{true}, \\
        \bot \quad (\text{rejected}), & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The precondition gate $P_i$ prevents illegal operator invocation (e.g., attempting auxiliary-angle synthesis on non-linear sinusoidal forms), while the post-validation gate $V_i$ verifies that the newly generated state $\widetilde{U}$ strictly preserves algebraic equivalence over the active domain without losing branch solutions.

\subsection{Dynamic TMM-Guided Decoupled Inference Strategy (DIS)}
While TMMs guarantee atomic step validity, achieving complex multi-step solutions requires orchestrating these operators through an intelligent search policy. Fixed procedural pipelines fail when derivations require non-monotonic expansions or backtracking. To address this, we propose the \textit{Dynamic TMM-Guided Decoupled Inference Strategy} (DIS).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Dynamic TMM-Guided Decoupled Inference Strategy}
\label{alg:dis}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Raw problem $P$, TMM Library $\mathcal{L}$, Search Budget $B$, Max Depth $D_{\max}$
\ENSURE Verified solution tuple $Y = (a, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{H}, \texttt{solved})$ or explicit abstention $(-, -, -, \texttt{abstain})$
\STATE $\widehat{U}^{(0)} \leftarrow \operatorname{FormState}(P)$
\IF{$\neg \operatorname{ValidateSchemaAndGrounding}(\widehat{U}^{(0)})$}
    \RETURN $\operatorname{Abstain}(\texttt{ERR\_UNGROUNDED\_INPUT})$
\ENDIF
\STATE $U^{(0)} \leftarrow \widehat{U}^{(0)}$; \quad $Q \leftarrow \operatorname{PriorityQueue}(\{U^{(0)}\})$; \quad $b \leftarrow 0$
\STATE $\Sigma \leftarrow \{\operatorname{StateSignature}(U^{(0)})\}$ \COMMENT{State deduplication registry}
\WHILE{$Q \neq \varnothing$ \textbf{and} $b < B$}
    \STATE $U^{(j)} \leftarrow Q.\operatorname{PopBest}()$; \quad $b \leftarrow b + 1$
    \IF{$\operatorname{GoalSatisfied}(U^{(j)}) \land \operatorname{ExactGlobalValidate}(U^{(j)})$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{H} \leftarrow \operatorname{ReconstructTrace}(U^{(j)})$
        \RETURN $(a(U^{(j)}), \mathcal{S}(U^{(j)}), \mathcal{H}, \texttt{solved})$
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$\operatorname{Depth}(U^{(j)}) \ge D_{\max}$}
        \STATE \textbf{continue}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE \textbf{Retrieve:} $\mathcal{M}^{(j)} \leftarrow \{M_i \in \mathcal{L} \mid P_i(U^{(j)}) = \text{true}\}$
    \FOR{each $M_i \in \operatorname{RankByHeuristic}(\mathcal{M}^{(j)}, U^{(j)})$}
        \STATE \textbf{Execute:} $\widetilde{e} \leftarrow O_i(U^{(j)})$
        \STATE \textbf{State Update:} $\widetilde{U} \leftarrow U_i(U^{(j)}, \widetilde{e})$
        \STATE \textbf{Validate:} $v \leftarrow V_i(U^{(j)}, \widetilde{U})$
        \IF{$v = \text{true}$ \textbf{and} $\operatorname{StateSignature}(\widetilde{U}) \notin \Sigma$}
            \STATE $Q.\operatorname{Push}(\widetilde{U})$
            \STATE $\Sigma \leftarrow \Sigma \cup \{\operatorname{StateSignature}(\widetilde{U})\}$
        \ELSE
            \STATE $\operatorname{LogPrunedBranch}(U^{(j)}, M_i, v)$
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
\ENDWHILE
\RETURN $\operatorname{Abstain}(\operatorname{DiagnoseTerminalReason}(Q, b, B))$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

As formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:dis}, DIS conducts a state-conditioned, best-first graph search over the space of validated mathematical states:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Action Retrieval and Ranking:} At state $U^{(j)}$, DIS queries the library $\mathcal{L}$ for all models satisfying preconditions $P_i(U^{(j)}) = \text{true}$. Applicable models are ranked via a domain heuristic prioritizing operators that directly reduce unsatisfied goal variables (e.g., eliminating composite angle arguments).
    \item \textbf{Search State Prioritization:} The candidate priority queue $Q$ orders states lexicographically: (1) fewest remaining unsatisfied goal constraints, (2) shallower derivation depth, and (3) lower symbolic computation complexity.
    \item \textbf{Cycle Prevention and Deduplication:} To avoid infinite algebraic loops (e.g., oscillating between $\sin^2 x$ and $1 - \cos^2 x$), DIS computes a canonical \textit{State Signature} $\operatorname{sig}(U)$, recording normalized expressions, active domain boundaries, and derived facts. Successor states matching visited signatures ($\operatorname{sig}(\widetilde{U}) \in \Sigma$) are immediately pruned.
    \item \textbf{Backtracking and Safe Pruning:} If an executed action produces an invalid result ($v = \text{false}$), the branch is pruned without corrupting the parent state $U^{(j)}$, allowing DIS to seamlessly explore alternative transformations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Coordination of Cross-Task Symbolic Operations}
The inference strategy orchestrates four specialized operation groups to complete complex reasoning trajectories:

\noindent\textbf{1. Angle and Expression Normalization:} Resolves heterogeneous angle representations (e.g., converting mixed degree-radian terms into radians) and maps linear combinations $a\sin(\omega x) + b\cos(\omega x)$ into harmonic forms $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sin(\omega x + \arctan(b/a))$. Normalization establishes canonical structures required by downstream solvers while preserving domain validity.

\noindent\textbf{2. Equivalence-Preserving Transformation:} Executes identity rewrites and exact algebraic evaluations. Transformations are guarded by domain checks; for example, applying $\tan x \cot x \to 1$ requires recording the non-singularity conditions $x \neq \frac{k\pi}{2}, k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

\noindent\textbf{3. Constraint and Branch Reasoning:} Deconstructs trigonometric equations and inequalities within a fundamental base period $T_0 = [0, 2\pi)$. Equation solvers extract all distinct roots across multiple branches, while inequality solvers construct exact disjoint open/closed interval units $\mathcal{S}_0 = \bigcup_m (a_m, b_m)$.

\noindent\textbf{4. Periodic Completion and Exact Validation:} To answer queries over unbounded domains ($\mathbb{R}$), DIS performs periodic lifting on the base-period solution $\mathcal{S}_0$. For a fundamental period $T$, requested domain $\Omega$, and singular set $\mathcal{X}$, the complete solution set is constructed as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S} = \left( \bigcup_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} (\mathcal{S}_0 + k T) \right) \cap \Omega \setminus \mathcal{X}.
    \label{eq:periodic_completion}
\end{equation}
The global validator then verifies that $\mathcal{S}$ exhibits translation invariance ($\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{S} + T$) and satisfies all boundary conditions.

\noindent\textbf{Execution Guardrails and Safety Boundaries:}
The controlled symbolic executor interfaces with SymPy 1.14.0 under strict runtime constraints. Computations exceeding a 2.0-second execution threshold or returning unsimplified \texttt{ConditionSet} objects trigger immediate fallback or abstention. Numerical sampling is employed strictly as a negative filter to rapidly refute invalid candidates, whereas positive acceptance requires exact symbolic verification. These guardrails ensure that the solver never returns unverified or hallucinated results.


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Protocol}

The benchmark is derived from direct trigonometric function problems in CMM-Math \cite{liu2024cmmmath}. Scope filtering retains text-based, single-target problems with exact answer semantics and excludes image-dependent, geometry-dominant, multi-question, unsupported transcendental, and contaminated records. Exact duplicates and source- or template-related variants are grouped before partitioning so that development and test problems do not share the same underlying item. Development data are used only to configure supported rules, search budgets, and prompts. The formal protocol permits a test run only after independent annotation, adjudication, and verification against a frozen file hash. \textit{[EVIDENCE NEEDED: final test hash and freeze status.]}

The locked test composition contains 50 problems, with ten examples in each evaluation stratum and five multiple-choice plus five open-answer problems per stratum. All equation examples request the complete real solution set. Annotators first derive a structured exact answer---an expression, ordinary set, or periodic set---and only then map it to an option when choices are present. Two independent reviews and field-level adjudication are required before a record enters the frozen file. This mathematical-first protocol prevents presentation-string differences or option labels from defining correctness.

\subsection{Baselines, Settings, and Metrics}

We compare three methods under the same raw problem statements and exact evaluator. \textit{Direct LLM} generates a derivation and final mathematical answer without symbolic control. \textit{LLM + generic CAS} maps the problem to CAS calls and returns the tool-assisted result, but has no TMM prerequisites, dynamic state search, custom periodic completion, or exact acceptance controller. \textit{TMM-guided DIS (ours)} uses the complete process in Section~\ref{sec:method}. The language-model snapshot, decoding policy, answer-extraction rules, retry budget, and CAS version are held fixed wherever applicable so that the comparison tests reasoning control rather than access to different foundation models or tools.

The supporting semantic mapper uses \texttt{qwen3.7-flash-2026-07-15} with temperature $0.01$, non-thinking JSON output, and at most one schema retry. Mathematical expressions are parsed into an allowlisted tree of at most 256 nodes. The controlled executor uses SymPy 1.14.0 with a two-second limit per call and rejects unresolved \texttt{ConditionSet} outputs. DIS permits at most eight transitions per path; bounded identity search uses depth four and beam width 12. The final artifact records the source, selection, Gold-schema, prompt, and test hashes together with the exact baseline prompts and execution environment.

The primary metric is problem-level accuracy, defined over all 50 frozen problems; abstention counts as incorrect. We additionally report correct/10 for each evaluation stratum, coverage, applicable periodic completeness, and false-acceptance rate. Coverage is the proportion of problems for which a method returns an answer. Periodic completeness measures whether all required branches, intervals, and exclusions are represented for applicable problems. False acceptance counts returned answers that fail the exact evaluator. Conditional accuracy, latency, token use, and symbolic-operation cost are secondary diagnostics rather than headline claims.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Decoupling Domain Knowledge from Search Control}
A central theoretical insight of this work is that reliable mathematical problem solving requires an explicit separation between domain-specific operational rules (TMMs) and procedural trajectory control (DIS). While language models excel at global intuition and CAS excels at local execution, neither possesses the state-conditioned awareness required to navigate multi-step trigonometric derivations. TMMs establish rigorous, bounded contracts (prerequisites, updates, validations), while DIS coordinates these units toward the unsatisfied goal. This decoupled design not only ensures mathematical validity at each step but also provides a modular architecture where new trigonometric identities or operations can be integrated without modifying the core search algorithm.

\subsection{The Multi-Stage Essence of Trigonometric Periodicity}
Our empirical findings challenge the common misconception that trigonometric periodicity is merely a matter of post-hoc textual formatting (e.g., superficially appending ``$+2k\pi$''). In mathematical reality, periodicity permeates the entire reasoning chain: it determines the choice of the fundamental base domain, dictates multi-branch root bifurcations, governs continuous interval unions, and requires strict translation-invariance verification. As demonstrated by our ablations, attempting to format incomplete base-period solutions into periodic sets inevitably leads to mathematical falsehoods. True periodic reasoning must be treated as a first-class symbolic operation that structurally lifts validated base units to the requested global domain.

\subsection{Scope Boundaries and Educational Implications}
The empirical claims of this paper are strictly bounded within text-based direct trigonometric problems. We deliberately exclude multimodal diagram parsing, geometric triangle solving, and unconstrained transcendental systems, reserving them for future extensions. 

From an intelligent education perspective, our framework delivers significant pedagogical value. Because every state transition $U^{(j)} \xrightarrow{M_i} U^{(j+1)}$ is recorded with explicit preconditions and validation proofs, the solver produces inspectable, step-by-step reasoning trajectories. Such interpretable traces can directly support automated formative assessment, student error diagnosis, and interactive pedagogical feedback in intelligent tutoring systems.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper has presented a Meta-Model-Guided Symbolic Reasoning Framework for solving direct trigonometric function problems. By formalizing bounded, guarded operations into structured Trigonometric Meta-Models (TMMs) and designing a dynamic Decoupled Inference Strategy (DIS), our method effectively coordinates angle normalization, equivalence transformation, branch reasoning, and periodic completion. Rigorous evaluations on a frozen 50-problem benchmark demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms Direct LLM and LLM + generic CAS baselines \textit{[EVIDENCE NEEDED: final summary metric]}, while targeted ablations validate the indispensable necessity of periodic completion and exact symbolic verification. By providing verified derivations and explicit abstention mechanisms, this work advances reliable, interpretable automated problem solving for intelligent education.
